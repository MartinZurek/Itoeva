Wide, calm and uplifting instrumental piece for pixel mountains on a
clear morning - snowy peaks, thin fresh air, an eagle circling far above
and a long path winding upward. 84 BPM in 3/4 with a steady, clearly
audible pulse on every beat from the first bar to the last.

A warm French horn plays one simple, noble four-bar melody in G major,
and the same melody returns for the whole piece. A fingerpicked acoustic
guitar plays the chords G major, E minor, C major, D major in a repeating
cycle, a cello answers the melody on every second phrase in the same
middle register, soft string pads hold the chords like distant ridges,
and a gentle frame drum with a round bass keeps the slow, walking pulse.

In the second half, a soft, rounded triangle-wave 8-bit voice answers
the melody with its own short phrase in the same middle register, like
mountain air drawn in pixels, taking turns with the lead rather than
playing along with it. Halfway through, a contrasting four-bar B phrase
at the same energy gives the main melody a rest - a different lead
instrument and a new chord colour from the same key - and then the main
melody returns. The sound is spacious, calm and warm, clear in the mid
range, never harsh.

Harmony and tone: one key from the first bar to the last, simple diatonic
chords in consonant voicings, every voice perfectly in tune with every
other. The lead and the keys stay in the warm middle register, and the few
high notes are soft and rounded. A clean, open tone with generous headroom
and a gentle, soft top end.

The piece starts at full level and keeps it: no intro, no ending, no
fade-out. The last bar leads straight back into the first so the 90-second
loop is seamless.

No vocals, no choir, no spoken word, no wind or bird sounds. No drops, no
risers, no dramatic climax, no epic percussion.
