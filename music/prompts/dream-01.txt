Vast, tender instrumental piece for a pixel world under a sky full of
stars - awe, longing and quiet hope, like looking up at night and missing
someone far away. 72 BPM in 4/4 with a soft, steady pulse on every beat
from the first bar to the last.

A warm pipe organ plays one simple, repeating pattern of broken chords in
E minor, and the same four-bar pattern returns for the whole piece. A soft
felt piano plays a slow, singable melody of long notes in the middle
register. Soft sustained strings hold the chords: E minor, C major, G
major, D major. A quiet wooden tick, like a small clock, marks every beat.

In the second half, a soft, rounded triangle-wave 8-bit voice answers the
melody with its own short phrase in the same middle register, like a
distant star blinking in pixels, taking turns with the lead rather than
playing along with it. Halfway through, a contrasting four-bar B phrase at
the same energy gives the main melody a rest - a different lead instrument
and a new chord colour from the same key - and then the main melody
returns. The sound is wide, warm and calm, clear in the mid range, never
harsh, with no deep rumble.

Harmony and tone: one key from the first bar to the last, simple diatonic
chords in consonant voicings, every voice perfectly in tune with every
other. The lead and the keys stay in the warm middle register, and the few
high notes are soft and rounded. A clean, open tone with generous headroom
and a gentle, soft top end.

The piece starts at full level and keeps it: no intro, no ending, no
fade-out. The last bar leads straight back into the first so the 90-second
loop is seamless.

No vocals, no choir, no spoken word. No drums, no drops, no risers, no
