Instrumental lo-fi jazz-hop for a quiet pixel-art living world late in the evening - the same warm family as its companion piece, but sparser and more nocturnal, built around a soft glockenspiel or music-box-like mallet instead of Rhodes electric piano in the lead.

Tempo around 70 BPM in 4/4, a touch slower and more spacious than its companion piece. Gentle glockenspiel or celesta states a simple, unhurried melody with long decay; a muted upright bass enters only on select beats rather than every measure; soft brushed drums stay mostly implied, more felt than heard. A warm, gently low-pass filtered clarinet or muted horn occasionally answers the mallet melody from further back in the mix. A little tape hiss and warm analog saturation for texture, but every instrument stays perfectly in tune and in time throughout - no pitch wobble, no wavering intonation, no warped-tape or dragged-vinyl effect, nothing that sounds like the recording is slowing down or stretching.

Calm, tender and quietly melancholic - like a house settling in for the night - but never heavy, sluggish or worn out, and reflective rather than sad. No vocals, no rap, no spoken word, no dramatic drops, no aggressive mastering, no cinematic climax, no busy percussion. Keep the dynamics even and leave generous space for game sound effects.

Structure it as background music for a living game world: an understated opening, a steady middle section with small organic variations, and a gentle ending that can crossfade naturally back into the beginning. It should feel handcrafted, warm and quietly memorable - a distinct companion piece to the other evening track, not a variation of the same melody, while never demanding the player's attention.
