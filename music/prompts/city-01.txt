Proud, melancholic instrumental piece for a pixel city - walking down a
busy street, unbothered and a little heartbroken, head held high. 86 BPM
in 4/4 with a steady, unhurried drum groove on every beat from the first
bar to the last.

A full string section plays one sweeping four-bar loop in F major, and the
same loop repeats for the whole piece: a bold, singing line that rises and
falls over the chords F major, C major, D minor, B flat major. Underneath,
a round electric bass follows the roots, a dry kick and a crisp snare on
two and four keep a laid-back hip hop pulse, and a clean electric guitar
holds one long, slowly swelling note.

In the second half, a soft, rounded triangle-wave 8-bit voice answers the
melody with its own short phrase in the same middle register, like the
same street seen in pixels, taking turns with the lead rather than playing
along with it. Halfway through, a contrasting four-bar B phrase at the
same energy gives the main melody a rest - a different lead instrument and
a new chord colour from the same key - and then the main melody returns.
The mix is wide and warm, clear in the mid range, never harsh.

Harmony and tone: one key from the first bar to the last, simple diatonic
chords in consonant voicings, every voice perfectly in tune with every
other. The lead and the keys stay in the warm middle register, and the few
high notes are soft and rounded. A clean, open tone with generous headroom
and a gentle, soft top end.

The piece starts at full level and keeps it: no intro, no ending, no
fade-out. The last bar leads straight back into the first so the 90-second
loop is seamless.

No vocals, no choir, no spoken word. No drops, no risers, no dramatic
climax.
