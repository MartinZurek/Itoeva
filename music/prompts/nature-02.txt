Sunny, open instrumental folk piece for a pixel meadow on a windy summer
day - freedom, fresh air and a light heart, walking through tall grass
toward the hills. 104 BPM in 6/8 with a light, bouncing pulse on every
beat from the first bar to the last.

A low whistle plays one simple, catchy eight-bar melody in D major, and
the same melody returns for the whole piece. A fingerpicked acoustic
guitar plays the chords D major, G major, B minor, A major in a repeating
cycle, a fiddle answers the melody on every second phrase, and a soft
frame drum with a round bass keeps the rolling rhythm.

In the second half, a soft, rounded triangle-wave 8-bit voice answers the
melody with its own short phrase in the same middle register, like the
wind drawn in pixels, taking turns with the lead rather than playing along
with it. Halfway through, a contrasting four-bar B phrase at the same
energy gives the main melody a rest - a different lead instrument and a
new chord colour from the same key - and then the main melody returns. The
sound is light, airy and warm, clear in the mid range, never harsh.

Harmony and tone: one key from the first bar to the last, simple diatonic
chords in consonant voicings, every voice perfectly in tune with every
other. The lead and the keys stay in the warm middle register, and the few
high notes are soft and rounded. A clean, open tone with generous headroom
and a gentle, soft top end.

The piece starts at full level and keeps it: no intro, no ending, no
fade-out. The last bar leads straight back into the first so the 90-second
loop is seamless.

No vocals, no choir, no spoken word, no bird sounds. No drops, no risers,
no dramatic climax.
