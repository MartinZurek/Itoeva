squeaking, a ball bouncing, pure fun and friendly competition, like a
nineties hip hop summer. 95 BPM in 4/4 with a tight, head-nodding boom bap
groove on every beat from the first bar to the last.

A funky slap bass plays one catchy two-bar riff in E minor, and the same
riff returns for the whole piece. A warm horn section answers every second
bar with short, punchy stabs. A clean electric guitar plays muted funk
chords on the off-beats over E minor 7 and A 7, and the drums are crisp
and snappy: fat kick, cracking snare on two and four, open hi-hat accents
and hand claps on the snare.

bass riff with its own short phrase in the same middle register, like a
scoreboard flashing in pixels, taking turns with the lead rather than
playing along with it. Halfway through, a contrasting four-bar B phrase at
the same energy gives the main melody a rest - a different lead instrument
and a new chord colour from the same key - and then the main melody
returns. The sound is punchy, warm and tight, clear in the mid range,
never muddy.

Harmony and tone: one key from the first bar to the last, simple diatonic
chords in consonant voicings, every voice perfectly in tune with every
other. The lead and the keys stay in the warm middle register, and the few
high notes are soft and rounded. A clean, open tone with generous headroom
and a gentle, soft top end.

The piece starts at full level and keeps it: no intro, no ending, no
fade-out. The last bar leads straight back into the first so the 90-second
loop is seamless.

No vocals, no rap, no spoken word, no crowd noise, no scratching. No
