Funky, confident instrumental streetball beat for a pixel court - sneakers
squeaking, a ball bouncing, pure fun and friendly competition, like a
nineties hip hop summer. 95 BPM in 4/4 with a tight, head-nodding boom bap
groove on every beat from the first bar to the last.

A funky slap bass plays one catchy two-bar riff in E minor, and the same
riff returns for the whole piece. A bright horn section answers every
second bar with short, punchy stabs. A clean electric guitar plays muted
funk chords on the off-beats over E minor 7 and A 7, and the drums are
crisp and snappy: fat kick, cracking snare on two and four, open hi-hat
accents and hand claps on the snare.

On every second pass a soft 8-bit square-wave voice doubles the bass riff
one octave higher, like a scoreboard flashing in pixels. The sound is
punchy, bright and tight, clear in the mid range, never muddy.

The piece starts at full level and keeps it: no intro, no ending, no
fade-out. The last bar leads straight back into the first so the 90-second
loop is seamless.

No vocals, no rap, no spoken word, no crowd noise, no scratching. No
drops, no risers, no solo.
